01 THE PERSON

Before the titles,
beyond the labels.

I have never quite believed that a person can be explained in a few lines.

You can tell someone what I study, what I do, or what I spend my time learning. You can mention the things I enjoy, the places I’ve been, or the interests that keep me curious. All of that might help you know a little about me.

But would it really tell you who I am?

I’m currently studying Biomedical Engineering at KUET. It’s an important part of my life, but it isn’t the whole of it.

There’s a side of me that finds expression in music. I enjoy singing, recording karaoke, and playing the ukulele. There’s another that gets drawn into films, stories, and the little details of visual storytelling.

Sometimes it’s a new place waiting to be explored; sometimes it’s nature, animals, a good game, or the opportunity to stand before an audience and share something.

And then there are all the things I haven’t discovered yet.

Perhaps that’s what makes me difficult to put into one category. My interests don’t always follow the same direction, and I don’t think they need to. I’m curious about different things simply because there’s something interesting to find in them.

I don’t want to be defined only by what I study, what I create, or what I might eventually become.

I’d rather be understood as a person who is still discovering what life has to offer, and figuring out what to make of it along the way.

Because there is always more to a person than the first thing you learn about them.— JOBAYER HAMJA

02 THE QUESTION

Life is a film.
Not a race.

In my story, I push back on the warning that if you don’t run, you’ll be crushed.

I ask who chose the race, and who we let direct the film. The film has one audience: you, because you see your life through your own eyes. Then comes the question that matters more than the crowd’s commentary: whose life is it, yours or theirs?

“Whose life is it? Yours or theirs?”

People will have something to say. But if I let “What will people say?” choose my direction, I may end up running a race that was never mine. Wanting to come first in the wrong race makes no sense. I want to choose the race before I try to win it.

That is the argument in the story. The joke at the end is that every director really likes their own film. The serious part is deciding who gets to direct yours.

03 THE WORLD

The point of view
changes the picture.

If the way we see a story matters, then I want to notice what each frame leaves in and leaves out.

Cinema draws me to its images, its storytelling, and the choices behind a visual narrative. Music gives me another way to express feeling; sometimes it is easier to sing than to explain. Nature, wildlife, animals, and unfamiliar places give me more to notice. Other people bring other perspectives, and I’m curious about what I might miss if I assume mine is the only one.

I like exploring unfamiliar subjects, too. Not because every question needs to become a plan, but because learning can change the way I see what I thought I already knew.

04 WHAT MATTERS

A life that still
sounds like mine.

The question “What will people say?” leads me back to what I want to keep hold of.

I want to be honest with myself about whether a choice is mine, or something I’m doing only because I’m afraid of the reaction. I believe individuality needs self-respect: I can listen to people without handing them authorship of my life.

Creativity matters to me because an idea can take a form: a melody, a visual story, a page. Learning matters because there is always something outside what I know. I don’t have a perfect rule for every decision. I can keep asking whether I’m being honest, whether fear is choosing for me, and what I still have to learn.

“This is my story, my choice.”

A line from the story · JOBAYER HAMJA

05 MANY SIDES, ONE PERSON

Not separate lives.
One wider story.

“Engineering student,” “singer,” “ukulele player” are all true. None is the whole introduction.

I can care about how something works and also care about how it feels. I can be interested in an unfamiliar subject, then spend time with a film, a melody, a place, or an idea that has no practical use beyond being worth my attention. I don’t need to turn every interest into an achievement to let it matter.

That mix is not a contradiction I need to solve. It is simply the range of things that make up my life beyond one label.

06 STILL BECOMING

THE STORY IS STILL OPEN

I don’t have
a final cut.

I’m still learning, evolving, making mistakes, and discovering who I am.

I can’t give you a finished version of myself, because I’m still becoming. For now, I want to keep choosing my own direction, stay curious about what I haven’t seen, and write the next part as honestly as I can.

Still learning. Still creating.
Still becoming.— JOBAYER HAMJA
